\section*{Summary}
MORTIMER combines monthly equal-risk-contribution (ERC) weights with a causal volatility overlay across six exchange-traded funds (ETFs). In the held-out out-of-sample (OOS) test, the portfolio returned 9.52\% annualised after five basis points of trading costs, with 7.47\% volatility, a 0.714 Sharpe ratio, a $-7.00$\% maximum drawdown, and annual half-L1 turnover of 1.119. Against matched unscaled ERC, volatility was 8.2\% lower and drawdown was 9.9\% smaller, while the Sharpe ratio declined from 0.827 to 0.714. Validation produced larger risk reductions and an annual return 15 basis points lower. MORTIMER reduced realised portfolio risk without improving risk-adjusted performance.

\section*{Economic hypothesis}
Investors accept equity, credit, duration, and commodity risk premia to hold exposures that diversify imperfectly across economic states. Benchmark mandates and periodic risk-budget adjustment can leave portfolios carrying exposure after market risk has risen. Large absolute and squared returns cluster, so recent returns contain information about near-term variance (Cont, 2001; Engle, 1982; Bollerslev, 1986). Realized-volatility methods use this persistence to forecast risk (Andersen et al., 2003), and volatility-managed portfolios vary exposure in response to those forecasts (Moreira and Muir, 2017). MORTIMER applies this principle to a monthly, cross-asset risk-balanced portfolio. It scales risky holdings down when forecast volatility exceeds a fixed target and holds the balance in cash. The hypothesis predicts lower realised volatility and drawdown after trading costs, with a return reduction small enough to preserve risk-adjusted performance. Because the forecast is lagged, exposure adjusts after volatility changes; reduced exposure also limits gains during recoveries.

\section*{Data and universe}
The fixed universe comprises QQQ, IWM, HYG, TLT, GLD, and DBC. Yahoo adjusted closes include distributions and splits; raw closes and volume support liquidity estimates (Yahoo Finance, n.d.). Cash accrues from initial-release observations in the Archival Federal Reserve Economic Data (ALFRED) DGS3MO series (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, n.d.-a, n.d.-b), using the next exchange session for availability and actual/365 day-count accrual. Factor returns use the Kenneth French data library (French, n.d.). The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) calendar spans 2 January 2008 to 2 October 2026, with 550 sessions reserved for estimation before scored returns. Training ends on 31 December 2018, and validation runs from 1 January 2019 to 1 October 2024. The held-out OOS period runs from 2 October 2024 to 2 October 2026, the shorter of the most recent 20\% of sessions and two years. The six funds were selected for continuous price and volume histories through the end of the sample; membership is fixed throughout the analysis.

\section*{Methodology}
At each month-end, estimate daily covariance from 126 completed observations using Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage toward a scaled-identity target (Ledoit and Wolf, 2004). The constrained equal-risk-contribution (ERC) allocation follows the risk-budgeting framework of Maillard, Roncalli, and Teïletche (2010) and solves
\[
\min_b\sum_{i=1}^{6}\left[b_i(\widehat\Sigma_t b)_i-\frac{b^\top\widehat\Sigma_t b}{6}\right]^2,
\qquad \mathbf 1^\top b=1,\quad 0\le b_i\le0.35.
\]
Here $b_i$ is the base weight, $\widehat\Sigma_t$ is daily return covariance, and $b_i(\widehat\Sigma_t b)_i$ is variance contribution. The 35\% name cap can constrain the optimiser from attaining equal contributions. Shrinkage addresses estimation error, which can impair optimised allocations out of sample (DeMiguel, Garlappi, and Uppal, 2009).

For current base $b_t$, calculate historical portfolio returns $x_s=b_t^\top r_s$ using observations through close $t$. An exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) with span $n$ applies geometrically declining weights to squared returns. The annualised volatility forecast and capped risky exposure are
\[
\widehat\sigma_t=\sqrt{252\max\{\operatorname{EWMA}_{20}(x_s^2),\operatorname{EWMA}_{63}(x_s^2)\}},\qquad k_t=\min(1,0.10/\widehat\sigma_t).
\]
Each estimate requires 63 observations. Taking the larger estimate combines a faster response with a slower volatility measure. A five-percentage-point exposure change triggers a trade, while monthly base rebalances proceed independently. Gross exposure cannot exceed one; the strategy is long-only and holds residual capital in cash.

The close-$t$ signal is executed at close $t+1$, after that session's return; new holdings first earn the next close-to-close return. This convention separates information formation, order execution, and the return earned by the new position. Given pre-fee risky holdings $h_i$, portfolio net asset value (NAV) $V$, desired weights $w_i$, and cost rate $c$, post-fee NAV satisfies
\[
V^{post}=V-c\sum_i|V^{post}w_i-h_i|.
\]
Costs are five basis points of risky dollars bought plus sold, representing spread, commission, and closing slippage; cash transfers carry no charge. A ten-basis-point cost case is evaluated on development data. The cash return proxy is derived from initial-release three-month Treasury bill yields.

\section*{Results}
We calculate annualised return as compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from split-local net wealth using 252 trading sessions per year. Annualised volatility is the sample standard deviation of daily returns multiplied by $\sqrt{252}$, and the Sharpe ratio uses daily excess returns relative to the cash return proxy (Sharpe, 1994). Maximum drawdown (MDD) is measured from initial wealth of one. Annual turnover is half the L1 change in risky and cash weights; transaction costs apply to risky purchases and sales. \section*{Risk management}
Base weights are long-only and capped at 35\% per instrument. Gross exposure cannot exceed 100\%, with the remainder held as cash. Holdings drift between executions, so realised weights can differ from target weights. The daily overlay trades when desired exposure changes by five percentage points; monthly base rebalances proceed independently. \section*{Liquidity and capacity}
\begin{samepage}
Liquidity is measured with previous-session trailing 20-session average daily dollar volume (ADDV). We express portfolio scale as initial assets under management (AUM), equal to net asset value at inception. Participation is $Q/ADDV$, where $Q$ is risky trade notional. The square-root impact scenario follows the empirical market-impact relation documented by Tóth et al. (2011):
\[
I=Y\sigma\sqrt{Q/ADDV},\qquad \text{NAV cost}=I\,Q/NAV,
\]
We set $Y=0.5$ and use lagged volatility $\sigma$ for each AUM scenario.

\section*{Limitations and further work}
The six-fund universe is defined by continuous price and volume histories through the end of the sample, conditioning membership on fund survival and data coverage. Point-in-time listings, closure records, and archived prices would incorporate launches, closures, and delistings as they became observable. Broader universes would test the allocation across additional equity, credit, and commodity markets.

The backtest charges a fixed five basis points on risky purchases and sales. Historical quotes, order records, and brokerage schedules would permit instrument-specific estimates of spreads, commissions, and slippage. Capacity scenarios use a square-root impact coefficient of $Y=0.5$; historical execution shortfall would permit estimation of this coefficient across order sizes and market conditions. The cash allocation accrues the initial-release three-month constant-maturity Treasury yield. A bill total-return index and institutional financing series would extend the comparison across investable cash vehicles.
